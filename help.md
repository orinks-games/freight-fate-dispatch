# Freight Fate: Dispatch

## The desk

You run the desk at a Freight Fate carrier. Each morning the load board fills, the roster shows who is free, and the clock sits at 6 AM. Time only moves when you act: assigning a load takes 10 minutes, a phone call 5, an interview 15, checking on a truck 2. Wait for what's next runs the desk to the next call, arrival or driver coming off a break. Close the day ends the shift at 6 PM; trips still rolling finish overnight, and the career saves at the start of each day.

## Freight

Loads come from two places. Customers are the carrier's contract accounts: each has lanes, a weekly volume and a contract rate, and tenders its loads a day ahead. Monday's tenders are on the desk the first morning; after that the phone rings at 5 PM with the next working day's, and Friday's are for Monday. Accept them all, go through them one by one on the Tenders screen, or decline them all. A tender left open at the close counts as declined. An accepted load leads the board on its day, named after the customer, with a delivery appointment; one still on the board at the close is a service failure the customer counts. Everything else on the board is spot freight from a broker. On the "who takes it" screen a spot load has one more row: counter the rate, which asks 10 percent more and takes 5 minutes. A broker who has had on-time loads from you says yes more often; a no sometimes gives the load to another carrier.

## The week

Day 1 is a Monday. Saturday's board is half size and Sunday's is three loads, with no tenders over the weekend. Sunday's close reads the week's scorecard: loads delivered, on-time percent, revenue, and each customer's tender acceptance and on-time record. Ninety percent on both holds an account and, after two good weeks, adds a load a week and a rate bump. Two weeks under 80 percent acceptance cuts a load a week; four weeks under loses the customer. The last row of the Tenders screen makes a sales call, once a day and 30 minutes, for a new account.

## Drivers

Each driver has five traits you learn by working with them: reliability, pace, care, communication and patience. A delivery sometimes teaches you one, faster in the first 90 days; a scale teaches you care and a traffic stop teaches you pace. An interview shows two. A driver's record and traits are the last row of their card on the roster. Goodwill is spoken as morale: solid, fine, grumbling or done. A driver who is done gives two weeks' notice; home time and a fair week can change their mind. A solid driver sometimes brings a friend to the applicant pool, with two traits already known. Every application carries the driver's record: citations in the last 3 years and how their inspections went. At 6 AM the trucks still out check in with where they are and when they land; a driver who goes quiet leaves you to check the truck yourself.

## The law

Hours of service are real: 11 hours driving, a 14-hour window, 70 hours in 8 days, a 34-hour restart. When a driver can't make an appointment on legal hours the desk warns you and lets you send them anyway. A scale will find that log. Any trip can be pulled into a roadside inspection: level 1 is the full truck and about an hour, level 2 a walk-around and 30 minutes, level 3 the driver and the log and 20 minutes. A clean inspection takes a point off the carrier's safety score; a violation adds points and a fine, and the driver pays moving and log fines while the carrier pays for its equipment. An hours violation at a scale is 10 hours out of service on the spot; an equipment order holds the truck for the repair. Fast drivers draw speeding stops. Citations stay on a driver's record for 3 years and on the carrier's score for 2, at half weight after the first year. Two serious violations in 3 years suspend a CDL for 60 days, three for 120; the driver parks when the load delivers. Medical cards last 2 years and a renewal costs a day. Once a quarter one driver draws a random drug test and loses the day. Over 30 points the carrier is audited: the desk opens at 10 AM, bad hours records cost a fine, and customers tender a quarter less until the score comes down.

## The phone

When the phone rings, press Enter to answer it from anywhere at the desk. Drivers call in sick, ask for home time, run out of hours, break down and wait at docks; brokers call with hot loads. Pick an answer with Up and Down and Enter. The caller waits until you do.

## Keys

Up and Down move through a menu, with wraparound. Home and End jump to the first and last item. A letter jumps to the next item starting with it. Enter chooses. Escape goes back. F1 reads help for the current item. Space, anywhere at the desk, says what needs you now. F2 stops speech.

## Settings

Settings on the start screen has the speech rate, a volume for each kind of sound including the music, whether menus say where you are, and the game controller. Online services are a separate item on the start screen, off until you turn them on.

## Files

Careers, settings and the session log live in a saves folder inside the game's folder on Windows and Linux, and in Application Support on a Mac. Importing a Freight Fate driver only reads the Freight Fate save.
