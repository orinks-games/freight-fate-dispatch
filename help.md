# Freight Fate: Dispatch

## The desk

You run the desk at a Freight Fate carrier. Each morning the load board fills, the roster shows who is free, and the clock sits at 6 AM. Time only moves when you act: assigning a load takes 10 minutes, a phone call 5, an interview 15, checking on a truck 2. Wait for what's next runs the desk to the next call, arrival or driver coming off a break. Close the day runs the rest of the day and ends the shift at 6 PM. It stops only when the phone rings, and carries on once you answer. Trips still rolling finish overnight. The career saves after everything you do, and Escape at the desk goes back to the start screen with nothing lost. If a contract load has no driver or a tender is still open, Close says so once; close again to go on.

## Freight

Loads come from two places. Customers are the carrier's contract accounts: each has lanes, a weekly volume and a contract rate, and tenders its loads a day ahead. Monday's tenders are on the desk the first morning; after that the phone rings at 5 PM with the next working day's, and Friday's are for Monday. Accept them all, go through them one by one on the Tenders screen, or decline them all. A tender left open at the close counts as declined, and so does a tender for today still open when its appointment time comes. Once tomorrow's tenders are accepted, the Tenders screen lists them, and Enter on one pre-plans a driver for it in 5 minutes, any time before you close the day. When the desk opens that truck rolls with no desk time spent, or the plan falls through and the briefing says why. An accepted load leads the board on its day, named after the customer, with a delivery appointment; one still on the board at the close is a service failure the customer counts. Everything else on the board is spot freight from a broker. On the "who takes it" screen a spot load has one more row: counter the rate, which takes 5 minutes. Every load has a ceiling the broker won't say. A counter that lands brings the rate up part of the way to it, further with a broker who trusts you; one that doesn't leaves the rate where it is, and sometimes the broker gives the load to another carrier. Brokers remember. On-time loads build a broker's trust and late ones spend it, and it fades by half each Sunday, so it stays with the brokers you keep hauling for. One who trusts you calls you first with a hot load. One whose load you drop after taking it pays you 8 percent under the board for a month. Each load reads its dock terms. A strict appointment means a late truck is worked in when the receiver has a door, one to three hours later, and the driver sits through it; any other load is first come, first served. Drop and hook means the driver swaps trailers and never waits at the shipper. A lumper fee is paid at the dock to unload: it comes out of a spot load, and goes on the invoice for a contract load. Some shippers are slow to load; once one has kept a truck waiting, its loads on the board say so. A driver who goes empty out on the road finds three local offers, one of them headed back toward the terminal; for a driver who goes home every night, each load on their own board says how far from home it ends.

## The week

Day 1 is a Monday. Saturday's board is half size and Sunday's is three loads, with no tenders over the weekend. Sunday's close reads the week's scorecard: loads delivered, on-time percent, revenue, and each customer's tender acceptance and on-time record. Ninety percent on both holds an account and, after two good weeks, adds a load a week and a rate bump. An account that grows to 4 loads a week, and again at 6, opens a new lane. Two weeks under 80 percent acceptance cuts a load a week; four weeks under loses the customer. The last row of the Tenders screen makes a sales call, once a day and 30 minutes, for a new account.

## Drivers

Each driver has five traits you learn by working with them: reliability, pace, care, communication and patience. A delivery sometimes teaches you one, faster in the first 90 days; a scale teaches you care and a traffic stop teaches you pace. An interview shows two. A driver's record and traits are the last row of their card on the roster. Goodwill is spoken as morale: solid, fine, grumbling or done. A driver who is done gives two weeks' notice; home time and a fair week can change their mind. Each driver has a home pattern: local drivers go home every night, regional drivers every weekend, and over-the-road drivers after two to three weeks out. The briefing says who is due home tonight. Enter on a driver on the roster offers to send them home: they drive empty to the terminal, and home time starts when they get there, 34 hours for a regional driver and two to four days over the road. A driver due home who is free at the terminal at the close goes home then, for the same time, unless you send them out. A missed home night costs goodwill. A driver whose home time is overdue and whose goodwill has run low calls to say they'll quit; after that call, one more missed home night with goodwill that low and they quit at the close, with no notice. Some drivers ask for something: no northeast, no reefer, or long runs only. It is on their application and their roster card, and a load that goes against it says so on their row; send them anyway and it costs a little goodwill. Sunday night each driver weighs the week's miles, which their roster card counts as the week goes: under 1,000 is a thin week and over 3,500 is run ragged, and both cost goodwill, while a fair week earns some. A driver home every night counts six tenths of that. A solid driver sometimes brings a friend to the applicant pool, with two traits already known. Every application carries the driver's record: citations in the last 3 years and how their inspections went. At 6 AM the trucks still out check in with where they are and when they land; a driver who goes quiet leaves you to check the truck yourself.

## The law

Hours of service are real: 11 hours driving, a 14-hour window that starts when the driver goes on duty, a 30-minute break after 8 hours driving, 10 hours off in a row before the next shift, and 70 hours in 8 days, which 34 hours off in a row restarts. A driver's roster card says their hours left on the 70 when it is under 15 or is what limits them, and when their restart is done; a load the 70 rules them out of says "Needs a 34-hour restart". When a driver would make an appointment only by driving past the legal clock, their row says "Short on hours", and Enter reads the warning first; a second Enter sends them anyway. A scale will find that log, and the driver takes 10 hours off when the load delivers. When even a straight run would get there after the appointment, the row says "Delivers late" instead; Enter says about how late, and a second Enter sends them on legal hours. Any trip can be pulled into a roadside inspection: level 1 is the full truck and about an hour, level 2 a walk-around and 30 minutes, level 3 the driver and the log and 20 minutes. A clean inspection takes a point off the carrier's safety score; a violation adds points and a fine, and the driver pays moving and log fines while the carrier pays for its equipment. An hours violation at a scale is 10 hours out of service on the spot; an equipment order holds the truck for the repair. Fast drivers draw speeding stops. Citations stay on a driver's record for 3 years and on the carrier's score for 2, at half weight after the first year. Two serious violations in 3 years suspend a CDL for 60 days, three for 120; the driver parks when the load delivers. Medical cards last 2 years and a renewal costs a day. Once a quarter one driver draws a random drug test and loses the day. At 30 points the carrier is audited: the desk opens at 10 AM, bad hours records cost a fine, and customers tender a quarter less until the score comes down.

## Money

Cash moves the way it does at a carrier. A delivered load is billed to its customer or broker, and brokers pay in 30 days, customers in 30 or 45. A new authority starts with factoring on: every load pays the next day for 3 percent. The Money screen switches it off, which keeps the 3 percent and waits for the money. With factoring off, the same screen turns on broker quick pay: spot loads pay in 2 days for 3 percent, and customers still take their 30 or 45. Fuel comes off the card at the pump at this week's diesel price, and every mile costs road money for tires, tolls and repairs. Contract loads add a fuel surcharge once diesel is over $3.50. Driver pay goes out in Friday's settlement. Each truck costs $40 a day for parking, the logging device and permits, taken at every close. The first of each 30-day month brings the truck notes, insurance priced by the safety score, and the terminal rent. Cash can run $40,000 below zero on the line of credit, at 1.5 percent a month; a month over the line and the bank calls it, which ends the career. Hiring, a truck, service and a second terminal can be paid for on the line, but not past it. So does a safety score of 60 for more than a week, or the last driver leaving. While either clock runs, the morning briefing says how many days are left. Every driver needs a truck. The Trucks screen, under Money, lists each one's age, miles and note; Enter asks, then a second Enter sends one to the shop for the rest of the day at $600; the last row buys a used tractor on a note. A truck past 15,000 miles since its last service breaks down three times as often, and hiring needs a spare truck.

## Growth

Reputation is the carrier's standing, and the Company screen reads it as a tier: a new authority, a known carrier at 60, an established carrier at 75, a preferred carrier at 90. Goals met, good customer weeks and a week at 90 percent on time raise it; uncovered loads, cut accounts, a receiver who won't move an appointment and a safety score over the audit line lower it. Each tier adds a spot load a day, an account the desk can serve, and bigger shippers on the sales call. From established, a customer with 30 loads behind it, 90 percent of them on time, and a clean run of weeks calls on a Monday about going dedicated: 10 loads a week at 5 percent over the contract rate, paid in 30 days, and they expect 90 percent acceptance and on time every week or they pull it after two. An uncovered dedicated load costs double. The last row of the Company screen opens a second terminal in one of four cities 250 to 900 miles out, for $10,000 and $4,000 a month more in rent, with an established reputation and five drivers. Two accounts ship out of it, its board fills each morning, and drivers go home to whichever terminal is nearer. Milestones arrive as news at the close: 100, 500 and 1,000 loads, 100 loads with one customer, a dedicated account, a driver with a year in, a truck paid off, a second terminal, preferred, six accounts, a full roster. The career score on Sunday's scorecard adds a point a delivery, another on time, 10 an account, 50 a milestone and 5 a point of reputation.

## The phone

When the phone rings, press Enter to answer it from anywhere at the desk. Drivers call in sick, ask for home time, run out of hours, break down and wait at docks; brokers call with hot loads. When a shipper holds a truck, billing detention brings in $150 and that shipper holds your trucks more often, more with each bill up to three. Waiving it costs the driver's goodwill, since they sit unpaid, and the shipper owes you one: its docks hold your trucks half as often until you bill it. Pick an answer with Up and Down and Enter; F1 on an answer says what it costs. The caller waits until you do.

## Tips

A tip is a sentence or two that starts with "Tip:" and plays once, the first time you reach something: a screen, a kind of row on a list, a kind of phone call, or a day like the first Friday. It comes after everything else that key says, with a short sound in front, and never holds up a key. The Tips row on the desk, after Briefing, lists the tips you have heard in the order you heard them; Enter or F1 on one says it again. The first row of the Tips screen switches tips on or off for every career. The last row, Hear all tips again, asks once, then forgets the tips this career has heard, so each plays again the next time you reach it. In the first week, when Space finds nothing that needs you, it names the first desk screen you haven't opened yet. A career saved before tips existed starts with every tip heard.

## Keys

Up and Down move through a menu, with wraparound. Home and End jump to the first and last item. A letter jumps to the next item starting with it. Enter chooses. Escape goes back. F1 reads help for the current item. Space, anywhere at the desk, says what needs you now: an empty driver first, then a contract load with no driver, then a load running late. Escape on the start screen asks once before quitting. F2 stops speech.

## Settings

Settings on the start screen opens on Speech, Sound and Display, each with its own list, and the game controller. Speech has the speech rate and whether menus say where you are. Sound has a volume for each kind of sound including the music. Press a category's first letter to jump to it, Enter to open it and Escape to come back. Settings also has whether Discord shows your friends what you are doing at the desk. Online services are a separate item on the start screen, off until you turn them on.

## Soundtrack

The start screen has its own theme. Three more pieces play at the desk: one through the morning, one through the afternoon, and one from 5 PM, arranged differently every time. Soundtrack on the start screen lists the ones you have heard and plays them on Enter.

## Files

Careers, settings and the session log live in a saves folder inside the game's folder on Windows and Linux, and in Application Support on a Mac. Keep the game in a folder you can write to; in a protected folder such as Program Files, careers can't be saved. A career changed outside the game is listed but won't open, and the file is left as it is. To bring careers from an older version on Windows or Linux, unpack this version over the older one's folder, or copy the older saves folder's careers folder into this copy's saves folder before starting a career here. Once this copy has saved a career, an older career copied in later won't open. On a Mac, older careers are already where this version looks. Importing a Freight Fate driver only reads the Freight Fate save.
